3. HAZARD 1: MOBILE PLANT AND EQUIPMENT
================================================================================

HAZARD DESCRIPTION:
Risk of struck-by incidents, crushing, entrapment, and collision involving
mobile plant, vehicles, and construction equipment.

CRITICAL CONTROLS:
□ Only operate mobile plant if trained and authorized
□ Only operate if equipment is safe, in good condition, used for intended purpose
□ Conduct walk-around check for people/hazards before operating
□ Stop operating if people fail to maintain safe distance
□ Keep safe distance from mobile plant; use pedestrian walkways
□ Never sleep or rest under/near mobile plant and equipment
□ Only operate if 360-degree visibility is maintained
□ Always wear seat belt when operating plant/equipment

KEY REQUIREMENTS:

Equipment Specifications:
- All mobile plant must have 360-degree vision capability or visibility aids
- See 1m high object from 1m away on all sides of vehicle
- Proximity Warning Alarm System (PWAS) required for operations near workers
- PWAS minimum specs: 360° coverage, in-cab alarms, monitors, employee tags
- Front and rear cameras with 120° angle and night vision capability
- Working lights, horns, reverse alarms, amber flashing beacons required
- Roll Over Protection Systems (ROPS) and Falling Object Protection (FOPS)
- Fully functioning air conditioning in all enclosed cabs
- Fire extinguisher on all mobile plant
- Plant/equipment/vehicles must not be older than 15 years
- Older equipment requires full manufacturer testing including NDT

Operational Controls:
- 3-meter exclusion zone from operating machines (minimum)
- No unauthorized persons within swing radius of excavators
- Engines switched off and keys removed when unattended
- Daily user inspections and weekly documented inspections required
- Quarterly Veri-fi inspections (QR code tracking system)
- No tracked equipment on access roads without protection measures

VERI-FI PROGRAM CATEGORIES:
- GREEN: Eligible - passed inspection, fit for site
- AMBER: Minor issues - must address within 7 days
- RED: Not eligible - cannot enter until rectified
- BLACK: Blacklisted - banned from all NEOM sites

PLANT CATEGORIES COVERED:
- Tipper Trucks
- Site Dumpers
- Compactors/Rollers
- Articulated Dump Trucks
- Dozers
- Mobile Concrete Boom Pumps
- Graders/Scrapers
- Loading Shovels
- Bobcats
- Excavators
- Tower Cranes
- Mobile Cranes
- Crawler Cranes
- Telescopic Handlers

4. HAZARD 2: DRIVING
================================================================================

HAZARD DESCRIPTION:
Occupational road risk including vehicle collisions, rollovers, pedestrian
incidents, and fatigue-related accidents.

CRITICAL CONTROLS:
□ Only operate vehicle with correct license
□ Never drive under influence of medication, drugs, or alcohol
□ Never drive with unsecure load
□ Always wear seatbelt; ensure passengers do the same
□ Never use mobile phone or handheld device while driving
□ Obey all signs, signals, and speed limits
□ Follow designated routes; no shortcuts
□ Regularly inspect and maintain vehicle

THREE-FACTOR RISK ASSESSMENT:
1. THE DRIVER
   - Competent and capable for safe driving
   - Properly trained and licensed
   - Clear instructions on staying safe
   - Sufficiently fit and healthy

2. THE VEHICLE
   - Fit for purpose
   - Inspected and maintained in safe condition

3. THE JOURNEY
   - Routes effectively planned
   - Realistic work schedules with sufficient time
   - Weather conditions considered

DEFENSIVE DRIVER TRAINING REQUIREMENTS:
- Project driving rules and regulations
- Driver responsibilities
- KSA country requirements and precautions
- Specific hazards: night driving, off-road, visibility, dust, wildlife, pedestrians
- Causes of accidents: fatigue, drowsiness, aggressive driving, substance abuse
- Risk awareness and route selection
- Vehicle familiarization and operation
- Inspection and maintenance procedures
- Reporting vehicle problems/defects
- Mobile phone restrictions
- Emergency and accident notification

SITE DRIVING RULES:
- Obey all signs/signals; never exceed speed limits
- Wear seatbelts; ensure passengers do the same
- No mobile phones or handheld devices
- Follow designated routes only
- No overtaking unless safe
- Use indicators for all maneuvers
- Maintain safe distance; no tailgating
- Reverse parking mandatory at all times

5. HAZARD 3: WORKING ON OR NEAR LIVE ROADS
================================================================================

HAZARD DESCRIPTION:
Risk of vehicle strikes, collisions with traffic, and incidents involving
workers and the public during roadworks.

CRITICAL CONTROLS:
□ Ensure awareness of all precautions in risk assessment
□ Never work outside specific work zone
□ Only cross live carriageway at controlled crossing points
□ Maintain clear line of sight with oncoming traffic
□ Never change temporary traffic management unless trained/authorized
□ Use designated work zone access/egress points
□ Always wear high visibility clothing
□ Report any concerns about temporary traffic management

KEY REQUIREMENTS:
- Robust engineering controls for segregation of workforce and vehicles
- Risk assessment addressing public protection
- Competent supervision with trained Supervisor always on site
- Physical barriers (not cones/tape alone) for excavations near roads
- Jersey barriers/water-filled barriers within 10m of roads
- Approved Traffic Management and Logistics Plan required
- Ministry of Transport coordination required
- Parking areas separated: commercial vehicles from private
- Reverse parking mandatory
- No vehicles on construction site access roads

6. HAZARD 4: BREAKING GROUND AND EXCAVATIONS
================================================================================

HAZARD DESCRIPTION:
Risk of trench collapse, striking underground services, falls into excavations,
and drowning from groundwater.

CRITICAL CONTROLS:
□ Only break ground with valid permit and awareness of precautions
□ Never break ground without service detection by locating device
□ Always use safe digging practices near buried services
□ Only enter excavation if sides are stepped, sloped, or supported
□ Only enter if protection prevents people/vehicles falling in
□ Only access if ramps, steps, or secure ladder in place

KEY REQUIREMENTS:

Permit to Dig System:
- All excavations require approved MS/RA and permit
- Permit must detail service detection method (CAT/Genny or GPR)
- Approved excavation profile identified
- Hold points documented
- Copy held by responsible Supervisor

Design Requirements:
- Excavations >1.25m deep require competent engineer design
- Design specific to ground conditions (soil sampling may be needed)
- Consider water table location and stability effects
- Surface water diversion required
- Prevent confined space risk in design

Edge Protection Standards:
- Edge protection for ALL excavations regardless of depth
- Minimum 1m setback from edge
- Cones/netlon/tape NOT acceptable as edge protection
- Stop blocks for vehicles near excavations
- Jersey barriers for excavations within 10m of roads
- Material bund walls for large groundworks

Access Requirements:
- Ramps, temporary stairs, or access bridges preferred
- Ladders as last resort only
- Ladders must be secured and extend 1m above landing
- Self-closing gates at ladder access points
- Escape routes for flooding emergencies

Inspection Requirements:
- Weekly documented inspection by competent engineer
- Daily inspection by competent Supervisor before work
- After any event affecting stability
- After material fall or injury incident

7. HAZARD 5: WORKING AT HEIGHT
================================================================================

HAZARD DESCRIPTION:
Risk of falls from height resulting in serious injury or fatality, and risk
of falling objects striking workers below.

CRITICAL CONTROLS:
□ Only work at height if trained
□ Never work at height without edge protection or harness fixed to anchor
□ Never enter exclusion zone established for work at height
□ Ensure tools and equipment are tethered
□ Maintain 3 points of contact when using ladders
□ Never modify/alter/dismantle edge protection unless trained/authorized
□ Only work at height with rescue plan and arrangements in place

HIERARCHY OF CONTROL (in order):
1. AVOID work at height (use extending equipment from ground)
2. PREVENT falls (work platforms, rope access)
3. REDUCE distance/consequences of falls

FALL PREVENTION PLAN REQUIREMENTS:
- Identification of all working at height tasks
- Standards for each scenario
- Identification of floor openings (lift shafts, risers, general openings)
- Clear use of hierarchy favoring collective over personal measures
- Training requirements for each task type

EDGE PROTECTION STANDARDS:
- Minimum 95cm height
- Toe board minimum 15cm, no gaps below
- No vertical gaps >47cm between rails or toeboard
- Continuous and robust construction
- Physical stops where mobile equipment used
- Inspected prior to use and after alteration/adverse weather
- Full height perimeter screens for 3+ floors

ACCESS EQUIPMENT REQUIREMENTS:

Scaffolding:
- Scaffold coordinator appointed for all arrangements
- Guard rails, mid-rails, toe boards on all open sides
- Closely boarded platforms
- Weekly inspection by competent person with valid 3rd party certificate
- Tag system at all access points

MEWPs:
- IPAF (or equivalent) operator training required
- Fall restraint worn in boom lifts
- Instructions and examination certificates in each MEWP
- Fire extinguisher in each MEWP
- Emergency descent arrangements required
- Tools tethered when working from MEWPs

Aluminum Towers:
- PASMA trained erectors
- Through-the-Trap (3T) or Advance Guardrail method
- Base to height ratio 3:1 outdoor, 3.5:1 indoor (max freestanding)
- Weekly inspection and record keeping

Ladders (Last Resort Only):
- Task must be low risk, short duration (<10 minutes), non-repetitive
- Not within 3m of additional fall hazard
- 1:4 angle ratio maintained
- Secured and extending 1m above landing
- Inspected prior to use

TOOL TETHERING:
- Risk assessment required for all work at height
- Tethers designed specifically for purpose
- Tools weighed to confirm within tether limits
- Heavier items tethered to fixed anchors (not persons)
- Required within 4m of leading edge

8. HAZARD 6: LIFTING OPERATIONS
================================================================================

HAZARD DESCRIPTION:
Risk of crushing, struck-by incidents, and fatalities from dropped loads,
crane failures, or rigging failures.

CRITICAL CONTROLS:
□ Only lift with approved lifting plan and when trained/authorized
□ Only use lifting equipment/accessories with suitable tags
□ Only lift if communication established with all involved/affected
□ Only lift if load is secure and test lift performed
□ Never exceed operating limits of equipment/accessories
□ Avoid walking/standing under suspended loads
□ Only lift when positioned on suitable stable platform
□ Only lift if equipment is inspected and maintained

KEY PERSONNEL:
1. APPOINTED PERSON - Overall authority for lifting safety
   - Plans all operations
   - Approves risk assessments, lift categorizations, method statements
   - Ensures lift team aware of content
   - Tests zoning/anti-collision systems daily
   - Controls deactivation authorization

2. CRANE SUPERVISOR
   - Directs and supervises lifting operations
   - Ensures operations only commence after lift plan approved
   - Supervises crane drivers and slingers/signalers
   - BS7121 compliant training required

3. SLINGER/SIGNALER (Rigger)
   - Red hard hat, orange hi-vis marked "Slinger/Signaler"
   - In attendance at all times crane is in operation
   - Recognized qualification required
   - Competency verified by on-site demonstration

LIFT CATEGORIES:
- BASIC: Established weight, no hazards/obstructions
- INTERMEDIATE: Established weight, hazards to consider, no multi-crane
- COMPLEX: Multiple cranes or load enhancement attachments

LIFT PLAN MINIMUM CONTENTS:
- Risk Assessment, Method Statement, Lift Plan (by certified AP)
- Appointed Person signature
- All personnel certificates attached
- Load details (weights, radius)
- Load analysis (weights, size)
- SWL analysis (% of crane capacity)
- Crane location drawing
- Lifting accessories list
- Laydown area for transport
- Barricading requirements
- Services locations
- Surrounding environment
- Valid 3rd party crane certificates

WEATHER RESTRICTIONS:
- No lifting when wind exceeds manufacturer threshold or gusts of 38 kph
- Out of service mode required at 45 kph or manufacturer limit
- Visibility must allow operator to see load or signaler
- Dedicated wind speed monitoring always required

CRANE REQUIREMENTS:
- SIL 2 anti-collision systems for multiple tower cranes
- Load chart displayed in operator cabin
- Fire extinguisher in cab
- Two drivers per single tower crane (minimum)
- Logbook completed daily
- Weekly inspection recorded

INSPECTION FREQUENCIES:
- Lifting accessories: 6-monthly 3rd party thorough examination
- Lifting appliances (cranes): 12-monthly 3rd party proof load test
- Man-riding equipment: 6-monthly examination
- Daily pre-use inspections by operator
- Weekly documented visual inspections

COLOR CODING SYSTEM:
- Q1: [Quarterly color 1]
- Q2: [Quarterly color 2]
- Q3: [Quarterly color 3]
- Q4: [Quarterly color 4]
Tags attached only to equipment with valid certificates

9. HAZARD 7: CONFINED SPACES
================================================================================

HAZARD DESCRIPTION:
Risk of asphyxiation, toxic atmosphere exposure, engulfment, and entrapment
in enclosed spaces with limited access/egress.

DEFINITION:
Any chamber, tank, vat, silo, pit, trench, sewer, flue, well, or similar
space where enclosed nature creates reasonably foreseeable significant risk.

CRITICAL CONTROLS:
□ Only enter with valid permit and awareness of precautions
□ Only enter if trained and authorized
□ Only enter if atmosphere tested and continuously monitored
□ Only enter if "top man" is in place
□ Only enter if communication established with all involved
□ Only enter if rescue plan in place and rescue equipment available

CONTRACTOR RESPONSIBILITIES:
- Supply all safety equipment (gas detection, escape breathing, harnesses, tripods)
- All equipment must be certificated and in good order
- Train all personnel entering confined spaces
- Employ Confined Space Permit System with emergency plan
- Conduct suitable and sufficient risk assessment
- Maintain effective supervision
- Provide PPE and emergency equipment
- Establish constant, intrinsically safe communications
- Conduct atmospheric monitoring before and during entry
- Develop emergency procedures including emergency first aid

DOCUMENTATION REQUIREMENTS:
- Schedule of all confined spaces
- Classification based on risk profile
- Roles and responsibilities with organization chart
- Risk assessment
- Access/egress arrangements
- Entry/exit system (Monitor, Register, ID Tag)
- Permit to work procedure and flow chart
- Atmospheric monitoring procedure and frequency
- Ventilation provisions and calculations
- Communication arrangements
- Equipment requirements (intrinsically safe)
- Fire prevention measures
- Rescue hierarchy:
  • Low hazard/one entrant: tripod rescue
  • Low hazard/multiple entrants: 15 min escape sets, 3rd party trained
  • High hazard/single: work rotation, escape sets, trained entrants
  • High hazard/multiple: SCBA, stand-alone rescue team
- Duration limits based on hazard level
- PPE schedule
- Fit test procedure if respiratory masks used
- Competency criteria (all must be 3rd party certified)
- Lighting requirements
- Signage schedule
- Stop work criteria

PERSONNEL SELECTION:
- Suitable size and fitness for task
- 6-monthly medical fitness test
- No lung disease, epilepsy, blood pressure conditions
- Experienced and trained (3rd party) in entry and rescue
- Suitable attendants confirmed by in-depth risk assessment

10. HAZARD 8: FIRE
================================================================================

HAZARD DESCRIPTION:
Risk of fire, explosion, burns, smoke inhalation, and structural collapse
from construction fire hazards.

CRITICAL CONTROLS:
□ Only smoke or use naked flames in designated areas
□ Keep work area tidy; regularly remove waste
□ Never misuse or overload electrical systems/equipment
□ Return gas cylinders and flammable substances to designated storage
□ Check electrical equipment regularly; replace faulty items
□ Never move or tamper with fire safety equipment/devices
□ Never block fire escape routes or emergency exits
□ Ensure awareness of emergency plans and arrangements

KEY REQUIREMENTS:

Fire Safety Standards:
- Comply with Saudi Building Code 801 for Fire Safety during Construction
- Detailed Fire Prevention Plan required
- No controlled or uncontrolled fires on site
- Strict housekeeping practices required
- Combustible waste removal daily
- No stacking against buildings/structures

Fire Protection Equipment:
- Extinguishers per 300 sq.m and within 23m travel distance
- Within 15m of flammable liquid/gas use (>2L or 2.3kg)
- In open storage yards within 23m travel
- At flammable/combustible storage areas
- At fuel dispensing/service areas
- On all motorized equipment
- Inspected per Saudi Building Codes and NFPA

Temporary Buildings:
- >3m clearance from other buildings (if outside)
- 1-hour fire resistance if inside another building
- Smoke detectors with flashing lights on exterior

Storage Requirements:
- Flammable materials in locked, ventilated areas external to buildings
- Not in direct sunlight
- >4m from buildings and boundary fences
- Fire extinguisher at storage location
- Quantities kept to minimum
- Returned to store end of each day
- Liquids in CE approved containers within bunded areas

Smoking Controls:
- ONLY in designated smoking areas
- Prohibited near combustibles, explosives, flammables
- Not in existing buildings, basements, car parks
- Not in company vehicles or vehicles on site
- Not in offices, rest areas, mess facilities, toilets

Detection and Alarms:
- Zoned fire alarms in buildings, fitouts, basements, temporary offices
- Manual alarms at all fire points and security posts
- Audible alarms in all areas; flashing lights in large/noisy areas
- Combined detector and alarm in all temporary facilities
- Weekly alarm testing

Means of Escape:
- Two alternative escape routes where possible
- Routes clearly identifiable, unobstructed
- Emergency lighting (15 lux minimum, 3-hour performance)
- Directional signs and exit points marked
- Fire doors where appropriate
- Daily walk-through by fire wardens

Fire Coordinators and Wardens:
- Trained and appointed (civil defense accredited)
- Fire coordinator ensures plan implementation
- Fire marshals complete daily checks
- Minimum 6-monthly fire drills

11. HAZARD 9: HOT WORKS
================================================================================

HAZARD DESCRIPTION:
Risk of fire, burns, toxic fume exposure, and explosion from welding, cutting,
grinding, and other heat-producing work.

DEFINITION OF HOT WORKS:
Burning, welding, grinding, flame cutting, heating, and other processes
producing sources of ignition.

CRITICAL CONTROLS:
□ Only undertake hot work with valid permit and awareness of precautions
□ Only undertake if trained and authorized
□ Only undertake if combustible materials removed or protected
□ Only undertake if "fire watcher" is in place
□ Only undertake if suitable fire extinguisher at hot work location
□ Only undertake if aware of emergency arrangements

KEY REQUIREMENTS:

Permit System:
- Hot Works Permit procedure for all types
- Site log maintained for issued permits and locations
- Acetylene use by exception only, permit controlled
- Follow-up checks minimum 1 hour after completion

Work Area Preparation:
- Remove all flammable and combustible materials
- Protect timber floors with non-combustible material
- Use non-combustible (flame-proof) screens
- Fire extinguisher and fire watcher present
- Appropriate PPE worn

Equipment Requirements:
- Proprietary fittings only on gas welding equipment
- Flash-back arrestors on all gas/oxygen cylinders
- Cylinders moved only on proprietary trolley with extinguisher
- Full screening for arc welding where access unrestricted

Prohibited/Restricted Items:
- Petrol operated plant requires PMC/Delivery Partner approval
- Portable halogen lamps prohibited as task lighting
- Unattended ignition sources (bitumen boilers, space heaters) prohibited
- Compressors must be sited in open, away from plant, not petrol driven

Fire Resistant Materials:
- LPS 1207 standard for temporary protection
- Use for building finishes, materials, plant protection
- Use for containment of materials delivered to site

12. HAZARD 10: ENERGIZED SYSTEMS
================================================================================

HAZARD DESCRIPTION:
Risk of electrocution, electrical burns, arc flash, and fire from contact
with or work on electrical systems.

CRITICAL CONTROLS:
□ Only work on energized system with valid permit and awareness of precautions
□ Only work if trained and authorized
□ Only work if all appropriate isolations are in place
□ Only work if test verified effectiveness of isolation
□ Only apply and remove own personal lock and tag
□ Always use correct tools and equipment
□ Ensure additional precautions when system cannot be isolated

KEY REQUIREMENTS:

Electrical Safety Plan:
- Project specific plan covering temporary and permanent installations
- Approved prior to installation of any electrical systems
- Competent electrical coordinator/duty holder appointed
- Comply with IEE Wiring Regulations (BS7671:2018)

Temporary Power:
- Transformers, distribution boards secured to prevent unauthorized access
- Earthed and inspected before use
- All switchgear freely accessible with isolating switch
- Main switchgear lockable in off position
- One main switch for all site power in emergencies
- Generators earthed, covered, integrally bunded
- All units marked with DANGER signs and highest voltage

Circuit Protection:
- All circuits have protection devices, regularly checked
- All sockets >110V protected by RCDs (30mA max, zero delay)

Cable Protection:
- Industrial type cables, sockets, connectors only
- Domestic type prohibited in construction areas
- Cables elevated to avoid trip hazards
- Armored cables in work areas or damage-prone areas
- Buried cables minimum 0.5m deep in protective duct

Tools and Equipment:
- All portable tools must operate at 110V, center-tapped, double insulated
- >110V tools require PMC/Delivery Partner approval and permit
- Battery-powered tools preferred
- PAT testing required with color coding (quarterly)
- Visual inspection before each use

Color Coding by Voltage:
- 110 Volts: [Yellow]
- 220 Volts: [Blue]
- 415 Volts: [Red]

LOCK OUT TAG OUT (LOTO):
- Required for all work on energized systems
- Minimum 2 padlocks on isolation switch
- Keys held by operator/maintenance/Supervisor
- Tags with name and reason for lockout
- LOTO procedure must cover:
  • Scope and definitions
  • Key personnel, roles, competencies
  • Permit to work controls
  • Planning, communication, coordination
  • LOTO equipment specifications
  • Isolation procedures for all equipment types
  • Completion and energy restoration procedures
  • Training requirements
  • Equipment register

Underground/Overhead Services:
- Identify all services within/adjacent to project boundaries
- Investigate de-energization/relocation options
- If work near live overhead lines unavoidable:
  • Physical barriers (goalposts) and signage
  • Minimum 6m clearance parallel to lines
  • 9m max passageway width under lines
  • Rigid non-conducting goalposts at crossings

13. HAZARD 11: TEMPORARY WORKS
================================================================================

HAZARD DESCRIPTION:
Risk of structural collapse, instability, and failure of temporary support
structures during construction.

DEFINITION:
Engineered solutions to support/protect existing structures or permanent works,
support plant/equipment, support excavation sides, or provide access.

CRITICAL CONTROLS:
□ Only erect/dismantle temporary works per design, drawing, specification, sequence
□ Ensure approved temporary works design is available
□ Ensure all materials in good condition and identified in design
□ Only load/strike with permit and awareness of precautions
□ Never overload temporary works
□ Never access unless safe
□ Never modify/alter/dismantle unless trained and authorized

EXAMPLES OF TEMPORARY WORKS:
- Temporary structures, office gantries, protected walkways
- Hoardings, external perimeter fencing, signage
- Temporary roads, piling mats, crane mats, ramps, bridges, barriers
- Edge protection
- Façade retention, temporary support during demolition
- Excavation supports, dewatering, underpinning
- Open excavation and rock cuts
- Tower crane foundations, grillages, ties
- Temporary staircases, access scaffold, mobile towers, access cradles
- Waste chutes
- Loading towers and gantries
- Temporary anchor points (harnesses, winch points)
- Hoist bases and ties
- Formwork, falsework, propping
- Temporary stability of precast/structural frames
- Preston platforms, cantilever works
- Temporary roofs

5 PRINCIPAL STAGES (BS5975):
1. CONCEPT: Consider solutions, accept preferred, assemble information
2. DESIGN: Designer delivers compliant solution, checked and approved
3. INSTALL: Cannot start without approval; checked for completeness
4. USE: TW does its job; monitored for design compliance
5. DISMANTLE: Permission given when TW has served purpose

KEY PERSONNEL:

Designated Individual (DI):
- Responsible for TW procedure establishment and maintenance
- Chief Engineer/Operations Director level for Contractors
- Senior Resident Engineer for PMC/Delivery Partner

Temporary Works Coordinator (TWC):
- Formally appointed in writing
- Must have no other duties or roles
- Coordinates all TW activities
- First point of contact between designer and site
- Controls hold points
- Registers and records all TW elements
- Can halt works when safety risk exists

Temporary Works Supervisor (TWS):
- Formally appointed in writing
- Assists TWC with delegated responsibilities
- Signs off permit to load/unload (within experience limits)

DESIGN CATEGORIES (BS5975):
- Category 0: Standard solutions only (site check)
- Category 1: Simple designs (design team check)
- Category 2: Complex/involved designs (independent individual check)
- Category 3: Complex/innovative designs (another organization check)

PERMISSION STAGES:
- Permission to Install: When design approved and MS understood
- Permission to Load/Bring into Use: When all installation complete
- Permission to Dismantle: When TW has served purpose

14. HAZARD 12: SAFE USE OF EXPLOSIVES AND BLASTING
================================================================================

HAZARD DESCRIPTION:
Risk of unplanned explosion, flying debris, toxic fumes, ground vibration damage,
and misfire incidents.

CRITICAL CONTROLS:
□ Follow all precautions in drill and blast design and risk assessment
□ Ensure explosives transported, stored, and handled safely
□ Only enter blast zone if trained and authorized
□ Withdraw to designated safe distance during blasting
□ Comply with all warning signs and signals
□ Follow all precautions in event of misfire
□ Ensure unwanted/surplus explosives safely disposed
□ Ensure awareness of all emergency arrangements

KEY REQUIREMENTS:

Competent Personnel:
- Competent person with sufficient knowledge/experience must be employed
- Responsibilities of Explosives Engineers, Supervisors, Shot Firers, Sentries
  detailed in writing
- BS 5607 compliance required

Documentation Required:
- Blasting Risk Assessment and Method Statement
- Blasting Plan (geology, PPV calculation, blast pattern, hole specs, etc.)
- Magazine Operation Plan
- Face mapping report
- Post blast record
- Temporary Rock Support Records
- Explosives Demand Plan
- Blasting Schedule
- Personnel list (names, nationality, IQAMA number)

Pre-Planning:
- Detailed survey of site and adjoining areas
- Ground vibration transmission assessment
- Utility and services impact assessment
- Photographic evidence of existing conditions
- Notification to all affected parties

Storage Requirements:
- Movement recorded in/out of stores
- Formal delivery acceptance procedure
- Detonators stored separately from explosives
- Explosive Certificate required for transfers
- Stores kept clean, rubber overshoes required
- No iron/steel implements in stores
- No naked lights within 25m
- Issuing restricted to authorized persons only

Transport Requirements:
- Suitable vehicles for type and quantity
- Mixed explosive restrictions met
- Vehicles/containers clearly marked
- Written load information kept on vehicle
- Only sufficient explosives for work drawn
- Direct route to shot holes

Signals and Warnings:
- Audible AND visual signaling systems
- Explained through induction, site rules, visible signs
- Sentries posted with clear instructions

Electrical Shot Firing:
- Detonator leads twisted until charging complete
- No contact with potential stray current sources
- Circuit continuity tested after all persons clear
- Exploder handle kept with shot firer at all times
- Post-blast inspection before allowing return

15. HAZARD 13: WORKING ON OR NEAR WATER
================================================================================

HAZARD DESCRIPTION:
Risk of drowning, cold water immersion, struck by watercraft, and isolation
incidents during marine or waterside operations.

CRITICAL CONTROLS:
□ Only work with valid permit and awareness of precautions
□ Only work if trained and authorized
□ Only work if rescue plan in place and equipment available
□ Follow buddy system
□ Use designated access/egress to watercraft
□ Only perform tasks in designated work zone
□ Always obey the boat coxswain
□ Always wear life jacket

KEY REQUIREMENTS:

Risk Assessment:
- Detailed assessment prior to any work near, on, or over water
- Daily weather forecast assessment (including sea forecast)
- Construction Manager decides work appropriateness
- Suspend immediately if weather changes

Safety Equipment:
- Lifebuoys at suitable locations with 30m lifeline
- Not exceeding 60m spacing
- Daily checks for lifebuoys and rescue lines
- Lifejackets for everyone at risk of falling in water
- Lifejackets inspected every 12 months
- Training in proper use and maintenance
- Lifejackets equipped with whistle and emergency light

Rescue Provisions:
- Rescue boat required where workers could be swept away
- Rescue boat where shore rescue impossible
- High powered search lights for night work
- Chemical light sticks for night workers

Work Area Setup:
- Warning signage at all water edges/boundaries
- Adequate lighting including adjacent water surface
- Platforms and guardrails inspected
- Boards secured against rising water/tides
- Immediate work area fenced if full fencing impossible

Operational Controls:
- No lone working near, on, or over water
- Good housekeeping priority near water edges
- All offshore workers trained in emergency procedures
- Marine work coordinated with local yacht clubs, fishermen, users

16. HAZARD 14: WORKING IN THE HEAT
================================================================================

HAZARD DESCRIPTION:
Risk of heat exhaustion, heat stroke, dehydration, and related illness from
exposure to high temperatures and solar radiation.

CRITICAL CONTROLS:
□ Monitor weather conditions and adjust work accordingly
□ Provide adequate hydration stations
□ Implement work/rest cycles based on Thermal Work Limit (TWL)
□ Ensure workers trained to recognize heat illness symptoms
□ Provide shaded rest areas
□ Schedule heavy work for cooler periods
□ Ensure access to emergency medical response

THERMAL WORK LIMITS (TWL):

Category 1 (TWL >140): Unrestricted Work
- Normal work pace
- Standard breaks
- General hydration

Category 2 (TWL 115-140): Caution
- Self-paced work
- 10-minute break per hour
- Increased hydration

Category 3 (TWL 100-115): Restricted
- Moderate work pace
- 15-minute break per hour
- Mandatory hydration checks
- Buddy system

Category 4 (TWL 80-100): High Risk
- Light work only
- 20-minute break per hour
- Supervised hydration
- Increased monitoring

Category 5 (TWL <80): Work Cessation
- Emergency or essential work only
- Maximum controls required
- Constant monitoring

WEATHER WORKING PLAN REQUIREMENTS:
- Summer working hours arrangements
- Storm, fog, high wind protocols
- Rain, hail, lightning procedures
- Cold weather working arrangements
- Acclimatization procedures for new workers

TRAINING AND COMPETENCE:
- Recognition of heat illness symptoms
- First aid for heat-related illness
- Buddy system procedures
- Use of cooling equipment
- Emergency response procedures
